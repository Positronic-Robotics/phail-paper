# PhAIL: Time-to-Success CDFs and Paired Tests for Real-Robot VLA Evaluation

## Abstract

We introduce PhAIL (Physical AI Leaderboard), a real-robot benchmark and evaluation methodology for vision-language-action (VLA) policies. The dominant practice — single aggregate scalars at sample sizes typically N≤25 per condition, almost universally without confidence intervals or paired tests — cannot statistically support the comparative ranking claims it routinely publishes. PhAIL replaces it with a methodology in which scalars are for *reporting* and CDF-level paired tests are for *inference*.

The core primitive is the time-to-success CDF, with hard failures (drops outside the workspace, safety stops) absorbed as T=∞ ghost events so the distribution jointly captures speed and completion. Standard scalars (UPH, success rate at τ, MTBF/A, completion %) are projections of this CDF. Each task's CDF is anchored cell-wise to a teleoperated-human reference, yielding macro-averaged %-of-teleop as a dimensionless headline scalar that aggregates across heterogeneous tasks. We further show that different aggregation rules over the same CDF can produce different rankings: pairwise stochastic dominance favors bimodal "fast-or-T=∞" distributions, while integrated metrics favor consistency. The disagreement is structural, not noise; no single scalar uniquely encodes operational priority.

PhAIL evaluates four frontier VLAs (OpenPI π₀.₅, GR00T N1.6, ACT, SmolVLA) on a Franka FR3 arm across four objects (Towels, Wooden spoons, Scissors, Batteries) at ~30 paired rollouts per (model, object) cell, 2-3× the field-modal depth. At this depth the top two inference models (OpenPI, GR00T) remain pairwise indistinguishable on every metric tested; the third is separable; the fourth clearly worst. Subsampling on our data shows that an integrated-CDF test (Kolmogorov-Smirnov) detects ranking differences on borderline pairs at N=20-30 paired rollouts per condition where binary success-rate-at-threshold cannot detect them within budget. On the closest pair, no metric resolves the ranking at N=30, consistent with formula-based power calculations of ~600-1500 paired rollouts per condition. The choice of test, not just the choice of N, determines what is resolvable.

All policies were fine-tuned with their default open-source recipes; we did not optimize per model. The contribution is methodological — comparison primitives, statistical-power requirements, an open evaluation framework — alongside open data, code, and reference implementations.
